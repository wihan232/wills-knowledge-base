# Relevancy of Ad-hominem in Modern Day

While Ad-hominem arguments have since existed as early as the [[history-and-origin-of-the-ad-hominem-fallacy-02|Ancient Greeks]], people continue to use the Ad-hominem argument today. In fact, it may be the most commonly used and most effective fallacy.

## Examples of Usage


### 2020 Presidential Debate

Both presidential candidates used the ad-hominem attack, particularly the [[the-individual-types-of-ad-hominem-argument-03|abusive type]], against one another.

Biden
>"This man has no idea what he's talking about"

Trump
>He's on "the wrong side of history" and he's "against God"


### Social Media Comments

Social media comments often resort to ad hominem arguments as they bring out significant emotional impact. A study in 2025 found a sample of comments and categorized them:


1. Implying that commenter is unreliable or naive: 

>@User NO, raising the debt ceiling pays bills mostly earmarked and spent during the Trump years. You should study up before posting what you don't understand


2. Accusing post of being unreliable or misleading:

>@User FAKE NEWS!!!!


3. Using labels to attack a commenter's character:

>@User Quacks like a RACIST republican
