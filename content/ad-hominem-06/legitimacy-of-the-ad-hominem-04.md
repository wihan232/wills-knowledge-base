### The Standard Treatment

Charles Leonard Hamblin's book, _Fallacies_ (1970), critiqued how modern textbooks and literature at the time loosely defined the term [[brief-concept-of-the-ad-hominem-fallacy-01|fallacy]]. In his view, most books before him defined fallacy to mean _seemly valid but is not so_, which contradicted how modern logic was viewed. In response to this, newer academic works began referring to this definition as "the standard definition of fallacies".

### Standard Definition of Fallacies

The standard definition of fallacies have been defined as having three conditions:

A fallacy
1. Is an argument
2. that is invalid
3. appears to be valid

Modern works have extensively approached each of the three conditions, finding questions towards the necessity of each statement. Today there has yet to be a framework that definitively answers what a fallacy is.
