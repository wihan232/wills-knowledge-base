## Significant Historical Figures

There have been many famous figures in the field of philosophy have contributed in analyzing and establishing the roots of what we consider to be the [[the-individual-types-of-ad-hominem-argument-03|types]] of ad-hominem fallacy.
### Aristotle
![[aristotle.jpg]]
Records of ad-hominem being explicitly mentioned have dated back as early as Ancient Greek periods with Aristotle laying out the groundwork for the concept of ad-hominem being a personal attack towards someone rather than refuting the argument itself. Aristotle was among the first scholars to deem the ad-hominem to be [[legitimacy-of-the-ad-hominem-04|fallicious]] in the pejorative sense in his work,  _Sophistical Refutations_.


## Locke

![[locke.jpg]]

Known for his writing in philosophical concepts, such as his book, Of the Conduct of the Understanding, and many more, Locke contributed to the definition of the ad-hominem fallacy. Locke was credited as coining the _ad_ arguments ( _ad hominem_, _ad verecundiam_, _ad ignorantiam_, and _ad judicium_). He was the first to use the term _argumentum ad hominem_ in his work _An Essay Concerning Human Understanding_.
