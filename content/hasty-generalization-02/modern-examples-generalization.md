## Relevance in Modern Day

Hasty generalization is one of the more subtle fallacies used in discourse. It's commonly referred to as over-generalizing conclusions based on incomplete evidence. It's a argumentative shortcut often used to mask details about a nuanced issue.
# Examples in Real Life

In June 2015, presidential candidate Trump remarked on the people immigrating to the U.S through the southern border:

>"When Mexico sends its people, they're not sending their best. [...] They're sending people that have lots of problems, and they're bringing those problems with us. They're bringing drugs. They're bringing crime. They're rapists. And some, I assume, are good people."

Here Trump makes a claim based off a generalized assumption that most Mexicans who move to the U.S commit crimes there. For this particular claim, fact-checkers have debunked this claim, find that first-generation immigrants commit crimes at lower rates than native born populations.


In 2020, presidential candidate Joe Biden makes a generalization about the percentage of "no very good people" in society.

>"Do we really think this is as good as we can be as a nation? I don't think the vast majority of people think that. There are probably anywhere from 10 to 15% of the people out there, they're just not very good people. But that's not who we are. The vast majority of people are decent. We have to appeal to that and we have to unite people, bring them together."

Biden was criticized for this broad claim for being based on unbacked statistics. Here he makes a broad assumption about the number of bad people in society.
