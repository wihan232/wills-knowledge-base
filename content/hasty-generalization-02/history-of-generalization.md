## Aristotle

![[Aristotle2.webp]]

One of the oldest known instances of [[concept-generalization|hasty generalization]] being mentioned was by Aristotle in the text, _Prior Analytics_, where Aristotle warns against making hasty logical conclusions, especially ones that generalize from one evidence. For example, it would be naive to assume that just because the wise man Pittacus is a good person, all wise people would be good, too.

Aristotle also expands on the idea of hasty generalization through an other fallacy he mention, the _secundum quid_ in his work, _On Sophistical Refutations_. He gives the example of erroneously interpreting the expression 'The Ethiopian is black' in absolute terms -- where the naive person assumes that the Ethiopian is black in all aspects.
