## Relevance in Modern Day

Considered to be one of the most, if not the most, widely used logical fallacy, the [[concept-straw|straw man argument]] remains a highly effective argumentative shortcut in persuasion, culture, and politics. 

Known colloquially as putting words into someone's mouth, the fallacy makes the opposition look weaker by creating a hypothetical weak position that can easily be attacked. And importantly, a non-existent opponent can't talk back against anyone.
## Real Life Examples

Discussing the war in Iraq, President George W. Bush uses the technique when discussing people who don't agree with him:

>“There's a lot of people in the world who don't believe that people whose skin color may not be the same as ours can be free and self‐govern. I reject that. I reject that strongly”

The straw man that Bush used creates a hypothetical argument that his critics believe that people with different skin color can't govern. By mischaracterizing the views of his critics, Bush makes his position appear logical and benevolent.
