## Concept of No True Scotsman Fallacy

The no true Scotsman, also named the appeal to purity, is a fallacy where the person making the argument modifies their claim in response to a counterexample by saying the counterexample is tautologically excluded. 

While the definition might sound a little bit more complex than the other fallacies mentioned in this knowledgebase, the fallacy becomes more clear when an example is provided:

>Person A: No Scotsman puts sugar on their oatmeal.
>
>Person B: My cousin Mario is a Scotsman, and they put sugar on their oatmeal.
>
>Person A: But no true Scotsman puts sugar on their oatmeal.

The fallacy is similar to the argumentative tactic of moving the goal post, wherein the arguer modifies their claim after evidence is provided against their argument.
